# Health & Safety Policy — Dutiva Canada Inc.

**Effective:** [Date]  ·  **Reviewed annually**
**Applies to:** all workers, on-site and remote, and contractors on Company
premises.

Ontario's Occupational Health and Safety Act ("OHSA") requires a written policy
at 6+ regularly employed workers; Dutiva Canada Inc. adopts it now as standard
practice.

## 1. Commitment

The Company is committed to a safe and healthy workplace. Everyone shares
responsibility: the Company provides a safe environment and information;
workers follow safe practices and report hazards.

## 2. Responsibilities

**The Company (represented by the CEO):**
- Provide a safe workplace, safe equipment, and the information, training and
  supervision needed to work safely.
- Take every reasonable precaution for worker protection (OHSA s. 25(2)(h)).
- Maintain this policy, review it annually, and post or share it with all
  workers.

**Managers:**
- Ensure workers know and follow safe practices; correct hazards promptly.
- Report and document incidents per section 4.

**Workers:**
- Work per the OHSA and this policy; use provided safety measures.
- Report hazards, injuries, near-misses and unsafe conditions immediately.
- Never disable safety features or work in a way that endangers anyone.

## 3. Remote and home offices

Remote workers must maintain a safe workspace: an ergonomic setup, clear exits,
undamaged cords/equipment, and a working environment that allows safe,
uninterrupted work. A home-office check may be requested for setup guidance —
it is advisory, not an inspection regime.

## 4. Incidents and hazards

1. **Immediate danger:** move to safety; call 911 if urgent.
2. **Report:** tell your manager or the CEO as soon as possible — hazards,
   injuries, near-misses.
3. **Document:** manager records what happened, when, who was involved, and the
   corrective action taken; kept in the incident log.
4. **Critical injuries** (as defined by the OHSA): preserve the scene, notify
   the Ministry of Labour as required, and do not disturb the site beyond what
   is needed to save life or relieve suffering.

## 5. Right to refuse unsafe work

Workers may refuse work they reasonably believe is unsafe (OHSA s. 43). Report
the refusal to a manager; the situation is assessed with the worker, and no
reprisal follows a good-faith refusal.
