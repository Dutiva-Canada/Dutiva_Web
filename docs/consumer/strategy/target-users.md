# Target Users

## Primary users

Individual Canadians, aged 18–65, who are managing a personal administrative situation and do not know the steps, deadlines, or documents involved.

### Key segments

| Segment | Situations | Primary need |
|---|---|---|
| **Working Canadians** | Starting a job, losing a job, resigning, requesting accommodations, pay/record issues. | Understand rights and obligations, draft letters, keep records. |
| **Renters** | Repair requests, lease disputes, moving out, deposits, landlord notices. | Document problems, write formal correspondence, know local rules. |
| **Consumers** | Refunds, warranties, cancellations, service disputes, complaints to regulators. | Escalate effectively, preserve evidence, generate complaint letters. |
| **People in transition** | Moving provinces, getting married, separating, having a child, handling a death. | Map overlapping obligations, find the right agencies, organize documents. |
| **Gig / self-employed workers** | Invoicing, HST/GST basics, client disputes, record keeping. | Track obligations, store contracts and receipts, prepare correspondence. |
| **Family helpers** | Adult children helping parents with estate, housing, or medical paperwork; parents helping young adults. | Organize and share documents safely without becoming a legal representative. |
| **Newcomers** | Navigating Canadian federal/provincial systems, understanding tenancy/employment basics. | Plain-language orientation with clear source links and province-specific guidance. |

## Secondary users

- **Trusted helpers** invited by the account holder (spouse, adult child, advisor). Access should be optional, revocable, and audited; the product should not confuse helper access with professional representation.
- **Small service providers** (landlords, freelancers) who need to manage their own administrative records. The product is consumer-first, but some document templates may also be useful to very small operators.

## User assumptions

- The user is a non-lawyer and non-expert in administrative procedure.
- The user may be stressed, time-constrained, and accessing the product on mobile.
- The user may not know which jurisdiction applies.
- The user may not have all relevant documents at hand.
- The user may be using French as a first language or as a second language in a legal context.

## What users are trying to avoid

1. Missing a deadline because they did not know it existed.
2. Sending a document that is wrong for their province or situation.
3. Losing evidence because they did not organize it.
4. Paying for professional help for something they could handle themselves.
5. Failing to get professional help soon enough because the product overstated its capabilities.

## Accessibility and inclusivity

- Mobile-first but desktop-friendly.
- Screen-reader and keyboard accessible.
- Plain language, minimal jargon.
- Bilingual from the start.
- Support for users who may be in distress (crisis resources, warm tone, no pressure).
