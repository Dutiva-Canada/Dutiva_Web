# Positioning

## Positioning statement (draft)

For Canadians facing personal administrative life events, this product family is a trustworthy, bilingual, privacy-first guide that turns confusing situations into clear next steps, helps draft the right correspondence, and keeps the evidence organized — without pretending to be a lawyer, a government agency, or a substitute for professional advice.

## Value pillars

| Pillar | What it means for users |
|---|---|
| **Canadian first** | Built for Canada's federal/provincial/territorial landscape; never assumes one province's rule applies everywhere. |
| **Plain language** | Complex procedures explained in ordinary words; legal jargon used only when necessary and always explained. |
| **Action-oriented** | Every situation becomes a list of things the user can do, with context, deadlines, and document suggestions. |
| **Privacy and trust** | Sensitive documents and personal situations are protected; the product does not sell data or show ads. |
| **Honest boundaries** | Clear about what it can and cannot do; quick to recommend a qualified professional when appropriate. |

## Brand relationship to Dutiva

Three positioning options are documented. The final choice is an open business decision.

| Option | Branding | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Dutiva sub-brand** | e.g., "Dutiva — Personal" or a new consumer-facing name under dutiva.ca | Levers existing trust and infrastructure; cross-sell with employer product possible. | Consumer and employer audiences are different; legal/privacy terms must be separate; brand risk from any consumer incident spills over. |
| **2. Independent brand, same house** | Separate domain/app, clear "from the makers of Dutiva" lineage | Cleaner consumer identity; still benefits from trust transfer. | Requires separate marketing, support, and legal surfaces. |
| **3. Fully separate company** | New legal entity, new brand | Maximum liability separation; clean slate. | No trust transfer; highest setup cost. |

The documentation remains neutral on the choice. It does assume that generic infrastructure packages (i18n, UI, export guard patterns) can be reused if they remain Dutiva-controlled, and that any consumer-specific content is authored separately.

## Messaging do's and don'ts

**Do**

- "Helps you understand what to do next."
- "Drafts correspondence based on your answers."
- "Keeps your documents organized in one secure place."
- "Points you to official sources and professionals when needed."

**Don't**

- "Gives legal advice."
- "Files your claim for you."
- "Guarantees you win your dispute."
- "Replaces a lawyer / accountant / immigration consultant."
- "One-size-fits-all across Canada."

## Competitive differentiation

- **Jurisdiction precision**: most generic tools assume U.S. law or one Canadian jurisdiction; this product family does not.
- **Source provenance**: every rule points to a Canadian statute, agency, or tribunal where possible.
- **Evidence + documents + workflow integration**: most tools do one of these; the family is designed to connect all three.
- **Bilingual by design**: not a translated afterthought; French legal terminology is treated as a first-class concern.
- **Trust-first business model**: no ad-supported or data-selling model; monetization must be transparent and trust-preserving.
