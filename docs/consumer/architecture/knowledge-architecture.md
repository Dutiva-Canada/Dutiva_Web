# Knowledge Architecture

## Purpose

The products answer questions and guide workflows using authoritative Canadian legal and administrative sources. This document establishes the source hierarchy, provenance rules, and governance model so that the system never fabricates law, deadlines, rights, or procedures.

---

## Source hierarchy

When two sources conflict, prefer the higher-ranked source. Lower-ranked sources are used only when appropriate and are clearly labeled.

| Rank | Source type                            | Examples                                                                                                        |
| ---- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Government legislation and regulations | Acts, regulations, statutes, proclamations, consolidated law published by governments.                          |
| 2    | Official government agencies           | Service Canada, CRA, provincial employment standards offices, landlord-tenant boards, CNESST.                   |
| 3    | Courts and tribunals                   | Decisions of Canadian courts, tribunals, labour boards, administrative tribunals.                               |
| 4    | Official regulatory bodies             | CRTC, FCAC, CCTS, provincial regulators.                                                                        |
| 5    | Official public guidance               | Government fact sheets, guides, bulletins, frequently asked questions.                                          |
| 6    | Other authoritative sources            | Recognized legal information organizations, law societies (for general public information), accredited clinics. |
| 7    | Secondary sources                      | Reputable legal commentary, news, educational sites — used only when clearly labeled as background.             |

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-001`: The system SHALL prefer sources ranked 1–5 for any factual claim about law, rights, deadlines, or procedures.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-002`: Rank 6–7 sources SHALL be labeled as background or secondary and SHALL NOT be presented as authoritative legal basis.

---

## Source provenance

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-003`: Every knowledge item used in a workflow, answer, or document SHALL record:

- source title;
- source URL or identifier;
- source rank in the hierarchy above;
- jurisdiction(s) it applies to;
- effective date;
- last-reviewed date;
- review status (`valid`, `needs_review`, `superseded`);
- version or section reference where applicable.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-004`: The system SHALL NOT present unvetted or machine-curated citations as authoritative legal basis.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-005`: Knowledge items marked `needs_review` SHALL be withheld from user-facing authoritative citations until reviewed; they MAY be surfaced to internal reviewers or as "under review."

---

## Effective dates and supersession

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-006`: Every knowledge item SHALL have an `effective_date` and SHALL be checked against known amendments.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-007`: When a source is superseded, the system SHALL:

- mark the old item as `superseded`;
- link it to the new item (`superseded_by_id`);
- stop using the old item in new answers and documents;
- surface a notice if a user has saved content that referenced the old item.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-008`: Statutory figures (deadlines, dollar amounts, percentages, durations) SHALL NOT be presented without an effective date and a source citation, and SHALL NOT be generated by a language model.

---

## Conflicting sources

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-009`: When authoritative sources conflict, the system SHALL:

- flag the conflict;
- present the conflict to the user in plain language;
- direct the user to the relevant official source(s);
- recommend consulting a qualified professional when the conflict affects a high-stakes decision.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-010`: When a federal law and a provincial/territorial law both apply, the system SHALL help the user determine which is primary for their situation rather than choosing silently.

---

## Citation format

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-011`: Citations SHALL be concise and readable by non-lawyers. Example formats:

- "_Employment Standards Act, 2000_, S.O. 2000, c. 41, s. 54" (English)
- "_Loi sur les normes du travail_, RLRQ c. N-1.1, art. 82" (French)
- "Canada Labour Code, Part III, R.S.C., 1985, c. L-2"

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-012`: Where a source is available online, a stable URL SHOULD be provided.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-013`: Citations SHALL be bilingual where both language versions are official.

---

## Human review

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-014`: Every knowledge item SHALL have a review status and a reviewer attribution.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-015`: Knowledge items involving statutory figures, deadlines, or rights SHALL be reviewed by a qualified Canadian lawyer or the relevant government authority before being marked `valid`.

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-016`: A regular review cadence SHALL be defined (e.g., annually, or within 30 days of a known legislative change). See `governance/knowledge-governance.md`.

---

## Knowledge updates

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-017`: The system SHALL have a change-management process for knowledge updates:

- detect proposed changes (legislative monitoring, user feedback, legal review);
- queue the change for review;
- update the knowledge item and its effective/last-reviewed dates;
- mark prior version superseded if needed;
- notify product/engineering of any template or workflow impacts;
- record the change in a changelog.

---

## AI and knowledge

`AI-REQ-001`: A large language model SHALL NOT author statutes, regulations, deadlines, dollar thresholds, or government procedures from its parametric memory.

`AI-REQ-002`: AI MAY retrieve and summarize vetted knowledge items; the retrieval result SHALL include the source ID so the system can verify it against the vetted knowledge base.

`AI-REQ-003`: AI MAY suggest that a knowledge item is missing or that a situation requires a source not yet in the knowledge base; such suggestions SHALL be routed to human review, not presented as fact.

---

## Public content rule

`KNOWLEDGE-REQ-018`: Public-facing articles, guides, and marketing content SHALL NOT contain statutory figures, deadlines, or dollar thresholds. They MAY name the statute, describe the shape of the rule, and point to the official text. This mirrors the Dutiva rule for `/guides` and `/blog` articles.

---

## Knowledge base scope

The consumer knowledge base is separate from Dutiva's employer-focused corpus. It covers personal administrative law and procedure, not HR compliance. It must be built from scratch for the consumer product family, even if some underlying statutes overlap (e.g., employment standards apply to both employers and employees, but the user's perspective and the relevant procedural steps differ).
